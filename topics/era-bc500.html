500 BCE · The Mahajanapadas

Sixteen great realms across the Gangetic plains; Magadha begins its rise.

The second urbanisation of India: iron tools, rice cultivation and trade created walled cities along the Ganga. Monarchies (Magadha, Kosala, Avanti) competed with oligarchic gana-sanghas such as the Vajjis and Shakyas. In the north-west, Gandhara and Hindush fell under the Achaemenid Persians. Shramana movements — Buddhism and Jainism — arose in this world.

Key events

c. 528 BCE · Buddha attains enlightenment

Siddhartha Gautama attains enlightenment under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya (traditional date), founding Buddhism. — Bodh Gaya

c. 518–513 BCE · Achaemenid Persians take the Indus valley

Darius I incorporates Gandhara and the Indus region (Hindush) into the Persian Empire, opening a route for Aramaic-derived scripts. — Gandhara

c. 483 BCE (traditional) · First Buddhist Council

Held at Rajagriha, the Magadhan capital, soon after the Buddha's passing, to recite the teachings. — Rajagriha (Rajgir)

c. 5th–4th c. BCE · Panini codifies Sanskrit

The grammarian Panini, from the Gandhara region, composes the Ashtadhyayi, among the most influential grammars ever written. — Shalatura (near Attock)

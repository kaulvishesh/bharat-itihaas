100 CE · Kushans, Satavahanas & the Roman trade

Silk Road kings in the north, a Deccan empire in the middle, monsoon trade in the south.

The Kushans, a Central Asian people, ruled from Bactria to Mathura and linked India to the Silk Road and Rome; Gandharan and Mathura art produced the first images of the Buddha. The Satavahanas dominated the Deccan, while the Shaka Western Satraps held Gujarat and Malwa. In the south, Tamil Sangam kingdoms traded pepper, pearls and cotton with Rome through Muziris and Arikamedu; the Periplus (c. 50 CE) describes these ports.

Key events

c. 50 · The Periplus describes Indian ports

An anonymous Greek merchant's guide to the Red Sea and Indian Ocean lists Barygaza, Muziris and other ports and what each traded. — Muziris

c. 127 · Kanishka's reign begins

The Kushan emperor patronises Buddhism and Gandharan art; the exact date of his accession is still debated by historians. — Purushapura (Peshawar)

c. 130 · Gautamiputra Satakarni defeats the Shakas

The Satavahana king claims, in a Nasik inscription, to have crushed the Western Satraps and restored the Satavahana name. — Nashik

c. 150 · Junagadh inscription of Rudradaman

One of the earliest long Sanskrit inscriptions records repairs to the Sudarshana lake after a flood. — Junagadh (Girnar)

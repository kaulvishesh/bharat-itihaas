1206 CE · Founding of the Delhi Sultanate

Ghurid conquests give birth to a sultanate that will rule north India for 320 years.

After Muhammad of Ghor defeated Prithviraj Chauhan at the Second Battle of Tarain (1192), his general Qutb-ud-din Aibak ruled Delhi. On Muhammad's death in 1206, Aibak became the first sultan of the "Mamluk" (slave) dynasty. Bakhtiyar Khalji's raids into Bihar and Bengal (c. 1200) ended Pala–Sena power, and Nalanda and Vikramashila declined. Meanwhile the Cholas were fading, and Hoysalas and Kakatiyas were rising in the south.

Key events

1192 · Second Battle of Tarain

Muhammad of Ghor defeats Prithviraj Chauhan, opening the Gangetic plain to Turkic rule. — Tarain (Taraori)

c. 1199 · Qutb Minar begun

Aibak begins the red-sandstone victory tower; it is finished by Iltutmish and later sultans. — Mehrauli, Delhi

c. 1200 · Nalanda sacked

Bakhtiyar Khalji's forces ravage the monastic centres of Bihar; Nalanda's decline follows. — Nalanda

1206 · Delhi Sultanate established

Qutb-ud-din Aibak becomes the first sultan of Delhi after Muhammad of Ghor's death. — Lahore / Delhi

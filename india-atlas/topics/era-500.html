500 CE · After the Guptas: Hunas & new kingdoms

Hun invasions break Gupta power; the Deccan and south fragment into rising dynasties.

Around 500 the Huna chiefs Toramana and Mihirakula swept into north-west and central India, shattering Gupta authority; Yashodharman of Malwa claimed victory over Mihirakula c. 528. In the Deccan the Vakatakas patronised the Ajanta murals before collapsing, while Kadambas, Western Gangas and Pallavas consolidated the south and the Chalukyas of Badami were about to rise (c. 543). Astronomy flourished at Ujjain under Varahamihira.

Key events

c. 510 · Hunas under Toramana in central India

The Eran boar inscription of the Huna chief Toramana attests Huna rule over parts of Malwa after the Gupta decline. — Eran

c. 505 · Varahamihira's Panchasiddhantika

The astronomer at Ujjain summarises five older astronomical systems, including Greek-influenced ones. — Ujjain

c. 528 · Yashodharman defeats Mihirakula

The Mandsaur pillar inscription records that Yashodharman humbled Mihirakula, ending the Huna threat in central India. — Mandsaur

c. 543 · Chalukya power founded at Vatapi

Pulakeshin I fortifies Vatapi (Badami), beginning the Chalukya dynasty that will dominate the Deccan. — Badami (Vatapi)

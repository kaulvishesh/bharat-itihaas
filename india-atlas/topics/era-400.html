400 CE · Gupta Empire

A "Golden Age" of science, literature, art and mathematics.

Gupta coins show kings as warriors, musicians and horse-sacrificers. Sanskrit became the court language of poetry and drama (Kalidasa). Mathematics and astronomy — decimal place value, zero as a number, the sine function — matured, and later transmitted to the Islamic world and Europe. Beyond the core, many neighbours were tributaries rather than provinces, so boundaries here are soft.

Key events

c. 400 · The Iron Pillar of Delhi

A rust-resistant wrought-iron pillar, dated by its inscription to the time of Chandragupta II, still stands today. — Mehrauli, Delhi

c. 399–412 · Fa-Xian travels across India

The Chinese monk Faxian spends years in Gupta lands collecting Buddhist texts and describes a prosperous, peaceful kingdom. — Mathura

c. 427 · Nalanda mahavihara founded

Kumaragupta I is traditionally credited with founding the monastic university of Nalanda, which later drew students from across Asia. — Nalanda

499 · Aryabhata writes the Aryabhatiya

The astronomer-mathematician, working at Kusumapura (Pataliputra), gives rules for place-value arithmetic, sine tables and a rotating Earth. — Pataliputra

Journeys and campaigns

  • Faxian's pilgrimage (c. 399–412) — The Chinese monk enters via Gandhara, visits Mathura, Kannauj, the Buddha sites and Pataliputra, then sails from Tamralipti to Sri Lanka.

1030 CE · Chola Empire at its peak

A maritime power projecting from Tamil country to Bengal and Southeast Asia.

The Cholas controlled the Kaveri delta, annexed northern Sri Lanka and in 1025 raided Srivijaya across the Bay of Bengal. In the north, Mahmud of Ghazni raided repeatedly (17 times by tradition) from Ghazni, annexing Punjab. Chola bronzes, Tamil literature and temple-centred economies defined an era; village assemblies recorded in the Uttaramerur inscriptions show elected local administration.

Key events

1010 · Brihadisvara Temple completed

Rajaraja I's great Shiva temple, topped by a 66 m granite vimana, is consecrated in the Chola capital. — Thanjavur

1025 · Gangaikonda Cholapuram founded

Rajendra I founds a new capital to commemorate his victorious march to the Ganga, and sends a naval expedition against Srivijaya. — Gangaikonda Cholapuram

1025–26 · Mahmud of Ghazni raids Somnath

The Ghaznavid ruler sacks the wealthy Shiva temple on the Gujarat coast. — Somnath

c. 1030 · Al-Biruni writes on India

The scholar, who accompanied Mahmud's campaigns, composes the Kitab al-Hind — a remarkable study of Indian science, religion and customs. — Punjab (Ghaznavid India)

Journeys and campaigns

  • Rajendra Chola's Ganga expedition (c. 1021–25) — The Chola army marches north through Kalinga and Odisha to the Ganga in Bengal.
  • Mahmud's raid on Somnath (1025–26) — From Ghazni through Multan and the Thar desert to the Saurashtra coast.
  • Chola naval expedition to Srivijaya (1025) — Across the Bay of Bengal from Nagapattinam to Kedah and Srivijaya.

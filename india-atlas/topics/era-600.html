640 CE · Harsha, Chalukyas & Pallavas

A north Indian empire under Harsha, balanced by powerful southern kingdoms.

India after the Guptas was a field of regional powers. Harsha of Kanyakubja ruled the Ganga–Yamuna heartland; the Chalukyas of Badami and the Pallavas of Kanchi fought for the Deccan and the south-east. Temples began to be built in stone: rock-cut at Mahabalipuram, structural at Aihole and Pattadakal. Xuanzang's memoir is the best surviving eyewitness account.

Key events

c. 618–634 · Pulakeshin II halts Harsha

The Chalukya king stops Harsha's southward advance at the Narmada river, fixing the north–south frontier. — Narmada frontier

c. 637–642 · Xuanzang studies at Nalanda

The Chinese pilgrim spends years in India, and his Records of the Western Regions become a key source for the period. — Nalanda

642 · Pallavas sack Vatapi

Narasimhavarman I defeats and kills Pulakeshin II and takes the Chalukya capital. — Badami (Vatapi)

7th c. · Rock-cut Mahabalipuram

Pallava kings carve monolithic rathas and reliefs on the Coromandel coast; the Shore Temple follows in the 8th century. — Mahabalipuram

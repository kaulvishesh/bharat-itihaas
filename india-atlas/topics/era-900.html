900 CE · Pratihara zenith & the Chola revival

Kannauj's empire at its widest as a new power rises in the Kaveri delta.

Mihira Bhoja and Mahendrapala made the Pratiharas the dominant north Indian power, a barrier against Arab expansion beyond Sindh and Multan. The Rashtrakutas still ruled the Deccan; in 916 Indra III even sacked Kannauj. In the south the Pallava–Pandya contest ended when Vijayalaya's heirs, Aditya I and Parantaka I, laid the foundations of Chola imperial power. The Hindu Shahis guarded the north-west.

Key events

c. 850 · Vijayalaya takes Thanjavur

A Chola chief captures Thanjavur from the Mutharaiyars, founding the dynasty's imperial line. — Thanjavur

c. 903 · Aditya I defeats the Pallavas

Chola king Aditya I defeats Aparajita Pallava, ending the Pallava dynasty and taking Tondaimandalam. — Tirupurambiyam

c. 915 · Al-Masudi visits India's west coast

The Baghdad-born historian travels through Sindh, Gujarat and the Konkan and records the kingdoms of the Balhara (Rashtrakuta) and the Pratiharas. — Khambhat (Cambay)

c. 916 · Indra III sacks Kannauj

The Rashtrakuta king raids the Pratihara capital, weakening the empire that had resisted the Arabs for two centuries. — Kannauj

300 BCE · Rise of the Mauryas

Chandragupta unites the Ganga plain, then faces — and bargains with — Seleucus.

After Alexander's brief incursion (326 BCE), Chandragupta Maurya overthrew the Nandas and absorbed the Indus region. His agreement with Seleucus I Nicator c. 305 BCE traded eastern satrapies for 500 war elephants. Megasthenes, the Seleucid envoy, left an account of Pataliputra, a city with a timber palisade of 570 towers.

Key events

326 BCE · Battle of the Hydaspes

Alexander defeats Porus on the Jhelum; his troops later refuse to march further east. — Jhelum (Hydaspes)

c. 321 BCE · Chandragupta founds the Maurya dynasty

With the counsel of Kautilya (Chanakya), Chandragupta overthrows the Nandas and takes Pataliputra. — Pataliputra

c. 305 BCE · Treaty with Seleucus Nicator

Chandragupta secures lands west of the Indus in exchange for 500 elephants; the two courts exchange envoys. — Paropamisadae (Hindu Kush)

Journeys and campaigns

  • Alexander's Indian campaign (327–325 BCE) — From Taxila to the Hydaspes, then down the Indus to the sea; his army refused to go further east after the Beas.

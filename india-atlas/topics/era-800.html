800 CE · The Tripartite Struggle

Pratiharas, Palas and Rashtrakutas fight for the prize of Kannauj.

For two centuries three dynasties contested Kannauj, the symbolic capital of north India. Pratiharas held the west and Rajasthan, Palas the Bengal–Bihar east, and Rashtrakutas the Deccan. Meanwhile Arabs held Sindh (from 711), and southern kingdoms — Pallavas and Pandyas — rivalled each other; the Cholas would soon overtake both. Monasteries like Vikramashila and Somapura flourished under Pala patronage.

Key events

711–712 · Arab conquest of Sindh

Muhammad bin Qasim defeats Raja Dahir; Sindh becomes the easternmost province of the Umayyad Caliphate. — Debal / Sindh

c. 756–773 · Kailasa Temple at Ellora

Rashtrakuta king Krishna I begins a monolithic temple carved top-down from a basalt cliff. — Ellora

c. 788–820 · Adi Shankara

The philosopher of Advaita Vedanta is traditionally placed here; he is said to have founded four mathas across India. — Kalady, Kerala

c. 800 · Vikramashila founded

Dharmapala of the Pala dynasty founds a monastic university that rivals Nalanda. — Vikramashila
